## Why

The instruction loop already existed, but only for a job's tailored CV. Opening it to the Master CV means the guardrail has to work the other way round, and that is the whole content of this PR.

**Tailoring** asks *"is this already on the CV?"* — correct, because there the Master CV is the entire universe of truth. **Editing the Master CV** is where new truth legitimately enters: a project finished last week is not on the CV yet, and refusing it for that reason would make the feature useless.

So the owner of truth changes. The user is not lying to themselves; the risk is the *model* inserting something they never said. The check is therefore **provenance, not vocabulary**.

Widening the vocabulary was the tempting fix and is the wrong one: it licenses *tokens*, not *claims*. "Add my Kafka project" would license the word Kafka and leave the model free to invent everything around it.

## Mechanism

**The instruction is the licence; `source_quote` is the citation.** Additive ops (`new_bullets`, `new_entries`) must cite words that genuinely appear in the instruction — the same mechanism inbox sync already applies to email. Both now share one normaliser in `jobpilot/textnorm.py` rather than growing a second notion of "appears in".

Licensing from the quoted span *alone* was implemented first and is wrong in practice: an entry's facts are spread across a sentence, so quoting the clause about the crawler rejected the project's own name. The quote's job is to be a citation a human can check in the diff.

## Numbers get their own check

The existing lexical guard cannot see them at all — its token regex requires a leading letter. Measured against the sample CV before designing anything:

| Text added to a CV | Old guard |
|---|---|
| "experienced with **Terraform**" | 🛑 caught |
| "increased throughput by **40%**" | ✅ **passed** |
| "**led a team of 12 engineers**" | ✅ **passed** |
| "handling **millions of orders** daily" | ✅ **passed** |

Survivable while a plan could not write such a sentence. Not survivable now that one can — an invented metric is the most damaging thing that can land on a CV.

## Schema shape was forced by the provider

The first design was one flat op with a discriminator, chosen to avoid `anyOf` across providers. Anthropic rejected it: `400 Schema is too complex`. Bisecting against the real API showed the cause is **optional fields, not size**:

| Schema | Bytes | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `TailorPlan` (control) | 4682 | PASS |
| flat op, with defaults | 3153 | FAIL |
| five lists, with defaults | 3291 | FAIL |
| five lists, **all required** | 3260 | **PASS** |

An optional field expands into an `anyOf`/nullable branch, and enough of those blow the budget however small the schema reads. The resulting shape — one small list per op kind, every field required — is also the better design: a `drop` can no longer carry a title.

## Review gate

- A refused edit left its `Run` row open forever: `session_scope()` rolls back on the way out, so the failure record was discarded and the row kept `finished_at=NULL`. On the Runs page a refused edit read as one *still running*. Pinned by a test that fails without the fix.
- Two ops could claim the same bullet, and `apply_edits` resolves indices against the original list — so the loser vanished silently. Refused in the guard rather than resolved by ordering, because ordering picks a winner without telling anyone.

## Known limits, stated rather than hidden

- **The guard is lexical.** It stops unknown technology names and unknown numbers. It does **not** stop invented prose assembled from licensed words — *"built a scalable crawler handling millions of listings"* contains no unknown token. The backstops are the mandatory diff and the `note` field; this is documented in `jobpilot/cv/CLAUDE.md`.
- **The UI has not been driven in a browser yet** — the new route needs a service restart, deferred by request. Endpoint, engine, guard and apply are all verified against real infrastructure.
- The Master CV is the corpus every later tailor is checked against, so this path is deliberately *stricter* than tailoring: a claim accepted here would be laundered into "already on the CV" for every job that follows.
